What this is

Argus is pre-release software in a private, invite-only beta. It is not finished. Things will break, and telling me when they do is the whole point of you being here.

Access is by personal invite code. Your code, the app and its builds are yours to use and nobody else's — please don't pass any of them on, publish screenshots or recordings, or describe how it works publicly without asking me first.

Argus prepares. You check it and you send it.

This is the most important thing on this page, so it is near the top.

Argus drafts emails, tailors CVs, writes letters, answers application forms and reads deadlines out of adverts. It does all of that with large language models, which are confidently wrong some of the time. They misread dates. They smooth a fact into something that is no longer true. Occasionally they state something your CV never said.

  • Argus never sends anything. It leaves finished email in your Drafts folder and stops there. Pressing send is always a person's decision, and it is always yours.
  • Argus never submits a form. The form filler fills fields in your browser. There is no Submit button in it, deliberately.
  • Nothing is marked as applied on your behalf.

Check every CV, letter, form answer and closing date before you use it. Anything you send goes out under your name and is your responsibility, not mine — including anything Argus got wrong.

It isn't advice

Argus is a tracking and drafting tool. It is not a careers adviser, recruiter, immigration adviser, lawyer or financial adviser, and nothing it produces is professional advice of any kind.

Scholarships, fellowships, visas and funded programmes carry their own declarations and eligibility rules. Read them yourself. A match score is Argus's guess about relevance; it is not a judgement about whether you qualify, and it has no standing with the organisation you are applying to.

No guarantees, including about deadlines

I make no promise that Argus will find opportunities, that its scoring is right, that a draft is any good, or that using it leads to an interview or an offer.

Deadlines and reminders deserve their own sentence. Argus reads closing dates out of adverts, posters and pages, which means it can read them wrong, miss them entirely, or fail to remind you — a laptop asleep, a scheduler that didn't run, a notification the operating system swallowed. Argus is not a safety net for a deadline that matters. Confirm anything important against the advert itself.

Your data stays on your computer

Everything you track lives in a database on your own machine. Your CVs are encrypted on disk and your API keys live in your operating system's keychain, not in a settings file. I have no copy of any of it and no way to read it.

A few things do leave your machine, and each one is listed — with what it contains and what triggers it — in the privacy notice. In short: an anonymous daily usage count, your name and email at activation so I know who is in the beta, and whatever you choose to put in a feedback report.

Your AI provider is your own

Argus runs on an AI model that you choose and, for the cloud ones, pay for with your own API key. When you pick Google Gemini or Anthropic Claude, the text Argus needs the model to work on is sent to that company under your agreement with them, not mine — and that text includes your CV, your career memory, the advert, and the letters and form answers being written.

Their terms and their privacy policy govern what they do with it. I don't see any of it and I'm not a party to it.

The bill is yours too. Argus spends against your key and I have no visibility of it and no way to cap it. Gemini's free tier covers ordinary use; Claude is paid. The thing that runs a bill up is a large batch — preparing twenty applications at once is twenty tailored CVs and twenty letters. Set a spending limit in your provider's own console.

If you would rather no outside company ever saw your CV, point Argus at a model running on your own computer (Ollama) in Settings → AI & models. Nothing leaves the machine in that mode. You can set it per capability, so your career memory can stay local while letters use a cloud model.

A local model is not free either — it is paid for in hardware. The model has to fit in this computer's memory alongside everything else, and the work runs on its processor: long jobs mean full load for minutes at a time, loud fans, a warm machine and a fast-draining battery. A model too large for the memory available will make the whole computer unresponsive until it is stopped. Argus checks each installed model against your machine and says which ones do not fit, but the choice and its consequences are yours. Modern computers throttle themselves rather than come to harm; even so, run long jobs plugged in and not on a bed or a sofa.

The builds are not code-signed

An Apple Developer ID costs $99 a year and this project doesn't have a budget. So macOS will show a Gatekeeper warning the first time you open Argus, and Windows will show SmartScreen.

That is expected, and the installation instructions tell you how to get past it. It also means your operating system is not vouching for the build — you are trusting it because you know where it came from. Download it only from the official releases page.

Feedback

Feedback you send reaches me by email and carries your app version, operating system and which AI provider you have configured, so a bug report is debuggable. An error report also attaches the last few lines of the app's log, with anything that looks like a credential masked out; ordinary feedback never does, because logs can mention pages you captured.

Ideas and suggestions you send me I may use to improve Argus, without obligation or payment. Nothing you send stops being yours.

Ending it

Either of us can end your access at any time and for any reason. I might, if the beta closes or a code is being shared; you might, by simply stopping.

If access ends, your data does not go anywhere — it is on your computer, and Settings → Privacy & security will export all of it as a zip, including from the locked screen. Deleting the app's data folder removes it for good.
